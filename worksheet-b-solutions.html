Formula sheet (every identity used on this page)

Sum and difference

$\displaystyle \sin(u \pm v) = \sin u\cos v \pm \cos u\sin v$
$\displaystyle \cos(u \pm v) = \cos u\cos v \mp \sin u\sin v$
$\displaystyle \tan(u \pm v) = \frac{\tan u \pm \tan v}{1 \mp \tan u\tan v}$

Double angle

$\displaystyle \sin 2u = 2\sin u\cos u$
$\displaystyle \cos 2u = \cos^2 u - \sin^2 u = 2\cos^2 u - 1 = 1 - 2\sin^2 u$
$\displaystyle \tan 2u = \frac{2\tan u}{1 - \tan^2 u}$

Power reducing

$\displaystyle \sin^2 u = \frac{1 - \cos 2u}{2}, \qquad \cos^2 u = \frac{1 + \cos 2u}{2}, \qquad \tan^2 u = \frac{1 - \cos 2u}{1 + \cos 2u}$

Half angle (sign from the quadrant of $\frac{u}{2}$)

$\displaystyle \sin\frac{u}{2} = \pm\sqrt{\frac{1 - \cos u}{2}}, \qquad \cos\frac{u}{2} = \pm\sqrt{\frac{1 + \cos u}{2}}$
$\displaystyle \tan\frac{u}{2} = \frac{1 - \cos u}{\sin u} = \frac{\sin u}{1 + \cos u}$

Product to sum

$\displaystyle \sin u\sin v = \tfrac{1}{2}[\cos(u - v) - \cos(u + v)]$
$\displaystyle \cos u\cos v = \tfrac{1}{2}[\cos(u - v) + \cos(u + v)]$
$\displaystyle \sin u\cos v = \tfrac{1}{2}[\sin(u + v) + \sin(u - v)]$
$\displaystyle \cos u\sin v = \tfrac{1}{2}[\sin(u + v) - \sin(u - v)]$

Sum to product

$\displaystyle \sin u + \sin v = 2\sin\left(\frac{u + v}{2}\right)\cos\left(\frac{u - v}{2}\right)$
$\displaystyle \sin u - \sin v = 2\cos\left(\frac{u + v}{2}\right)\sin\left(\frac{u - v}{2}\right)$
$\displaystyle \cos u + \cos v = 2\cos\left(\frac{u + v}{2}\right)\cos\left(\frac{u - v}{2}\right)$
$\displaystyle \cos u - \cos v = -2\sin\left(\frac{u + v}{2}\right)\sin\left(\frac{u - v}{2}\right)$

Section 1: Evaluating Using Identities

Find exact values. Break the angle into known angles, or use a half-angle or sum-to-product formula.

Pg 384 #19Sum/Diff
Find the exact values of the sine, cosine, and tangent of $\frac{13\pi}{12}$.
Formula used: $\sin(u \pm v) = \sin u\cos v \pm \cos u\sin v$, $\ \cos(u \pm v) = \cos u\cos v \mp \sin u\sin v$, $\ \tan(u \pm v) = \frac{\tan u \pm \tan v}{1 \mp \tan u\tan v}$
Show steps and answer

Split into known angles: $\frac{13\pi}{12} = \frac{9\pi}{12} + \frac{4\pi}{12} = \frac{3\pi}{4} + \frac{\pi}{3}$.

$\displaystyle \sin\frac{13\pi}{12} = \sin\frac{3\pi}{4}\cos\frac{\pi}{3} + \cos\frac{3\pi}{4}\sin\frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2} + \left(-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \frac{\sqrt{2} - \sqrt{6}}{4}$
$\displaystyle \cos\frac{13\pi}{12} = \cos\frac{3\pi}{4}\cos\frac{\pi}{3} - \sin\frac{3\pi}{4}\sin\frac{\pi}{3} = \left(-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)\frac{1}{2} - \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = -\frac{\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{6}}{4}$
$\displaystyle \tan\frac{13\pi}{12} = \frac{\tan\frac{3\pi}{4} + \tan\frac{\pi}{3}}{1 - \tan\frac{3\pi}{4}\tan\frac{\pi}{3}} = \frac{-1 + \sqrt{3}}{1 + \sqrt{3}} = \frac{(\sqrt{3} - 1)^2}{3 - 1} = 2 - \sqrt{3}$

Check the signs: $\frac{13\pi}{12}$ is in QIII, so sine and cosine are negative and tangent is positive.

$\displaystyle \sin\frac{13\pi}{12} = \frac{\sqrt{2} - \sqrt{6}}{4}, \quad \cos\frac{13\pi}{12} = -\frac{\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{6}}{4}, \quad \tan\frac{13\pi}{12} = 2 - \sqrt{3}$
✔ Book answer A209
Pg 395 #45Half Angle
Use the half-angle formulas to determine the exact values of the sine, cosine, and tangent of $\frac{3\pi}{8}$.
Formula used: $\sin\frac{u}{2} = \pm\sqrt{\frac{1 - \cos u}{2}}$, $\ \cos\frac{u}{2} = \pm\sqrt{\frac{1 + \cos u}{2}}$, $\ \tan\frac{u}{2} = \frac{1 - \cos u}{\sin u}$. The sign comes from the quadrant of $\frac{u}{2}$.
Show steps and answer

$\frac{3\pi}{8} = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{3\pi}{4}$, so use $u = \frac{3\pi}{4}$: $\cos u = -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, $\sin u = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$.

$\frac{3\pi}{8}$ is in QI, so all three answers are positive.

$\displaystyle \sin\frac{3\pi}{8} = \sqrt{\frac{1 - \left(-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)}{2}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 + \sqrt{2}}{4}} = \frac{\sqrt{2 + \sqrt{2}}}{2}$
$\displaystyle \cos\frac{3\pi}{8} = \sqrt{\frac{1 + \left(-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)}{2}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 - \sqrt{2}}{4}} = \frac{\sqrt{2 - \sqrt{2}}}{2}$
$\displaystyle \tan\frac{3\pi}{8} = \frac{1 - \cos u}{\sin u} = \frac{1 + \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}}{\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}} = \frac{2 + \sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2}} = \sqrt{2} + 1$
$\displaystyle \sin\frac{3\pi}{8} = \frac{\sqrt{2 + \sqrt{2}}}{2}, \quad \cos\frac{3\pi}{8} = \frac{\sqrt{2 - \sqrt{2}}}{2}, \quad \tan\frac{3\pi}{8} = \sqrt{2} + 1$
✔ Book answer A210
Pg 396 #83Sum → Product
Use the sum-to-product formulas to find the exact value of $\cos\frac{5\pi}{12} + \cos\frac{\pi}{12}$.
Formula used: Sum-to-product: $\cos u + \cos v = 2\cos\left(\frac{u + v}{2}\right)\cos\left(\frac{u - v}{2}\right)$
Show steps and answer
$\displaystyle \frac{u + v}{2} = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{6\pi}{12} = \frac{\pi}{4}, \qquad \frac{u - v}{2} = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{4\pi}{12} = \frac{\pi}{6}$
$\displaystyle \cos\frac{5\pi}{12} + \cos\frac{\pi}{12} = 2\cos\frac{\pi}{4}\cos\frac{\pi}{6} = 2\cdot\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \frac{\sqrt{6}}{2}$
$\displaystyle \cos\frac{5\pi}{12} + \cos\frac{\pi}{12} = \frac{\sqrt{6}}{2}$
✔ Book answer A211

Section 2: Rewriting Using Identities

Spot the pattern, then collapse or expand it with the matching identity.

Pg 384 #29Sum/Diff
Write the expression as the sine, cosine, or tangent of an angle: $\cos\frac{\pi}{9}\cos\frac{\pi}{7} - \sin\frac{\pi}{9}\sin\frac{\pi}{7}$.
Formula used: Pattern: $\cos u\cos v - \sin u\sin v = \cos(u + v)$
Show steps and answer
$\displaystyle \cos\frac{\pi}{9}\cos\frac{\pi}{7} - \sin\frac{\pi}{9}\sin\frac{\pi}{7} = \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{9} + \frac{\pi}{7}\right)$
$\displaystyle \frac{\pi}{9} + \frac{\pi}{7} = \frac{7\pi}{63} + \frac{9\pi}{63} = \frac{16\pi}{63}$
$\displaystyle \cos\frac{16\pi}{63}$
✔ Book answer A209
Pg 395 #53Half Angle
Use the half-angle formulas to simplify $\sqrt{\frac{1 - \cos 6x}{2}}$.
Formula used: Half angle with $u = 6x$: $\sqrt{\frac{1 - \cos u}{2}} = \left|\sin\frac{u}{2}\right|$
Show steps and answer
$\displaystyle \sqrt{\frac{1 - \cos 6x}{2}} = \left|\sin\frac{6x}{2}\right| = |\sin 3x|$

The absolute value is needed because a principal square root is never negative, but $\sin 3x$ can be.

$\displaystyle |\sin 3x|$
✔ Book answer A211
Pg 394 #21Double Angle
Use a double-angle formula to rewrite the expression: $6 - 12\sin^2 x$.
Formula used: Double angle: $\cos 2u = 1 - 2\sin^2 u$
Show steps and answer
$\displaystyle 6 - 12\sin^2 x = 6(1 - 2\sin^2 x)$
$\displaystyle = 6\cos 2x$
$\displaystyle 6\cos 2x$
✔ Book answer A210
Pg 396 #79Sum → Product
Use the sum-to-product formulas to write the difference as a product: $\cos\left(\theta + \frac{\pi}{2}\right) - \cos\left(\theta - \frac{\pi}{2}\right)$.
Formula used: Sum-to-product: $\cos u - \cos v = -2\sin\left(\frac{u + v}{2}\right)\sin\left(\frac{u - v}{2}\right)$
Show steps and answer
$\displaystyle \frac{u + v}{2} = \frac{2\theta}{2} = \theta, \qquad \frac{u - v}{2} = \frac{\pi}{2}$
$\displaystyle \cos\left(\theta + \frac{\pi}{2}\right) - \cos\left(\theta - \frac{\pi}{2}\right) = -2\sin\theta\sin\frac{\pi}{2} = -2\sin\theta$

The book writes the answer as $-2\sin\theta\sin\frac{\pi}{2}$; since $\sin\frac{\pi}{2} = 1$, it equals $-2\sin\theta$.

$\displaystyle -2\sin\theta\sin\frac{\pi}{2} = -2\sin\theta$
✔ Book answer A211
Pg 395 #69Product → Sum
Use the product-to-sum formulas to write the product as a sum or difference: $\sin(x + y)\sin(x - y)$.
Formula used: Product-to-sum: $\sin u\sin v = \frac{1}{2}\big[\cos(u - v) - \cos(u + v)\big]$
Show steps and answer
$\displaystyle u - v = (x + y) - (x - y) = 2y, \qquad u + v = (x + y) + (x - y) = 2x$
$\displaystyle \sin(x + y)\sin(x - y) = \frac{1}{2}(\cos 2y - \cos 2x)$
$\displaystyle \frac{1}{2}(\cos 2y - \cos 2x)$
✔ Book answer A211
Pg 394 #25Power Reducing (do #25 OR #27)
Rewrite the expression in terms of the first power of the cosine: $\sin^2 x\cos^2 x$.
Formula used: Power reducing: $\sin^2 u = \frac{1 - \cos 2u}{2}$, $\ \cos^2 u = \frac{1 + \cos 2u}{2}$
Show steps and answer
$\displaystyle \sin^2 x\cos^2 x = \frac{1 - \cos 2x}{2}\cdot\frac{1 + \cos 2x}{2} = \frac{1 - \cos^2 2x}{4}$

$\cos^2 2x$ is still squared, so power-reduce again: $\cos^2 2x = \frac{1 + \cos 4x}{2}$.

$\displaystyle = \frac{1}{4}\left(1 - \frac{1 + \cos 4x}{2}\right) = \frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{1 - \cos 4x}{2} = \frac{1}{8}(1 - \cos 4x)$
$\displaystyle \frac{1}{8}(1 - \cos 4x)$
✔ Book answer A210
Pg 394 #27Power Reducing (do #25 OR #27)
Rewrite the expression in terms of the first power of the cosine: $\sin^2 x\cos^4 x$.
Formula used: Power reducing: $\sin^2 u = \frac{1 - \cos 2u}{2}$, $\ \cos^2 u = \frac{1 + \cos 2u}{2}$. Product-to-sum: $\cos u\cos v = \frac{1}{2}\big[\cos(u - v) + \cos(u + v)\big]$
Show steps and answer
$\displaystyle \sin^2 x\cos^4 x = (\sin^2 x\cos^2 x)\cos^2 x = \frac{1 - \cos 4x}{8}\cdot\frac{1 + \cos 2x}{2}$

The first factor comes from #25.

$\displaystyle = \frac{1 + \cos 2x - \cos 4x - \cos 2x\cos 4x}{16}$

Product-to-sum on the last term: $\cos 2x\cos 4x = \frac{1}{2}(\cos 2x + \cos 6x)$.

$\displaystyle = \frac{1 + \frac{1}{2}\cos 2x - \cos 4x - \frac{1}{2}\cos 6x}{16} = \frac{1}{32}(2 + \cos 2x - 2\cos 4x - \cos 6x)$
$\displaystyle \frac{1}{32}(2 + \cos 2x - 2\cos 4x - \cos 6x)$
✔ Book answer A210

Section 3: Using Identities and Triangles

Draw the triangle in the given quadrant, find the missing side, then use the formula.

Pg 384 #41Sum/Diff
Find the exact value of $\sin(v - u)$, given that $\sin u = -\frac{8}{17}$ and $\cos v = -\frac{4}{5}$. (Both $u$ and $v$ are in Quadrant III.)
Formula used: $\sin(u \pm v) = \sin u\cos v \pm \cos u\sin v$, $\ \cos(u \pm v) = \cos u\cos v \mp \sin u\sin v$, $\ \tan(u \pm v) = \frac{\tan u \pm \tan v}{1 \mp \tan u\tan v}$
Show steps and answer

Triangle for $u$ (QIII): opposite $-8$, hypotenuse $17$, so adjacent $= -\sqrt{17^2 - 8^2} = -15$ and $\cos u = -\frac{15}{17}$.

Triangle for $v$ (QIII): adjacent $-4$, hypotenuse $5$, so opposite $= -\sqrt{5^2 - 4^2} = -3$ and $\sin v = -\frac{3}{5}$.

$\displaystyle \sin(v - u) = \sin v\cos u - \cos v\sin u$
$\displaystyle = \left(-\frac{3}{5}\right)\left(-\frac{15}{17}\right) - \left(-\frac{4}{5}\right)\left(-\frac{8}{17}\right) = \frac{45}{85} - \frac{32}{85} = \frac{13}{85}$
$\displaystyle \sin(v - u) = \frac{13}{85}$
✔ Book answer A210
Pg 395 #37aHalf Angle
Use the figure to find the exact value of (a) $\cos\frac{\theta}{2}$.
Formula used: $\sin\frac{u}{2} = \pm\sqrt{\frac{1 - \cos u}{2}}$, $\ \cos\frac{u}{2} = \pm\sqrt{\frac{1 + \cos u}{2}}$, $\ \tan\frac{u}{2} = \frac{1 - \cos u}{\sin u}$. The sign comes from the quadrant of $\frac{u}{2}$.
Show steps and answer

Hypotenuse: $\sqrt{8^2 + 15^2} = \sqrt{289} = 17$. The side adjacent to $\theta$ is $15$, so $\cos\theta = \frac{15}{17}$.

$\theta$ is acute, so $\frac{\theta}{2}$ is acute and its cosine is positive.

$\displaystyle \cos\frac{\theta}{2} = \sqrt{\frac{1 + \frac{15}{17}}{2}} = \sqrt{\frac{\frac{32}{17}}{2}} = \sqrt{\frac{16}{17}} = \frac{4}{\sqrt{17}} = \frac{4\sqrt{17}}{17}$
$\displaystyle \cos\frac{\theta}{2} = \frac{4\sqrt{17}}{17}$
✔ Book answer A210
Pg 395 #49Half Angle
Find the exact values of $\sin\frac{u}{2}$, $\cos\frac{u}{2}$, and $\tan\frac{u}{2}$, given $\tan u = -\frac{8}{5}$, $\frac{3\pi}{2} < u < 2\pi$.
Formula used: $\sin\frac{u}{2} = \pm\sqrt{\frac{1 - \cos u}{2}}$, $\ \cos\frac{u}{2} = \pm\sqrt{\frac{1 + \cos u}{2}}$, $\ \tan\frac{u}{2} = \frac{1 - \cos u}{\sin u}$. The sign comes from the quadrant of $\frac{u}{2}$.
Show steps and answer

Triangle for $u$ (QIV): opposite $-8$, adjacent $5$, hypotenuse $\sqrt{64 + 25} = \sqrt{89}$. So $\sin u = -\frac{8}{\sqrt{89}}$ and $\cos u = \frac{5}{\sqrt{89}}$.

Quadrant of $\frac{u}{2}$: divide $\frac{3\pi}{2} < u < 2\pi$ by $2$ to get $\frac{3\pi}{4} < \frac{u}{2} < \pi$, which is QII. Sine is positive and cosine is negative.

$\displaystyle \sin\frac{u}{2} = +\sqrt{\frac{1 - \frac{5}{\sqrt{89}}}{2}} = \sqrt{\frac{\sqrt{89} - 5}{2\sqrt{89}}} = \sqrt{\frac{89 - 5\sqrt{89}}{178}}$
$\displaystyle \cos\frac{u}{2} = -\sqrt{\frac{1 + \frac{5}{\sqrt{89}}}{2}} = -\sqrt{\frac{89 + 5\sqrt{89}}{178}}$
$\displaystyle \tan\frac{u}{2} = \frac{1 - \cos u}{\sin u} = \frac{1 - \frac{5}{\sqrt{89}}}{-\frac{8}{\sqrt{89}}} = \frac{\sqrt{89} - 5}{-8} = \frac{5 - \sqrt{89}}{8}$
$\displaystyle \sin\frac{u}{2} = \sqrt{\frac{89 - 5\sqrt{89}}{178}}, \quad \cos\frac{u}{2} = -\sqrt{\frac{89 + 5\sqrt{89}}{178}}, \quad \tan\frac{u}{2} = \frac{5 - \sqrt{89}}{8}$
✔ Book answer A211
Pg 394 #17Double Angle
Find the exact values of $\sin 2u$, $\cos 2u$, and $\tan 2u$ using the double-angle formulas, given $\sec u = -\frac{5}{2}$, $\frac{\pi}{2} < u < \pi$.
Formula used: $\sin 2u = 2\sin u\cos u$, $\ \cos 2u = \cos^2 u - \sin^2 u = 2\cos^2 u - 1 = 1 - 2\sin^2 u$, $\ \tan 2u = \frac{2\tan u}{1 - \tan^2 u}$
Show steps and answer

$\cos u = \frac{1}{\sec u} = -\frac{2}{5}$. Triangle for $u$ (QII): adjacent $-2$, hypotenuse $5$, so opposite $= \sqrt{25 - 4} = \sqrt{21}$ and $\sin u = \frac{\sqrt{21}}{5}$.

$\displaystyle \sin 2u = 2\sin u\cos u = 2\cdot\frac{\sqrt{21}}{5}\cdot\left(-\frac{2}{5}\right) = -\frac{4\sqrt{21}}{25}$
$\displaystyle \cos 2u = 2\cos^2 u - 1 = 2\cdot\frac{4}{25} - 1 = -\frac{17}{25}$
$\displaystyle \tan 2u = \frac{\sin 2u}{\cos 2u} = \frac{-\frac{4\sqrt{21}}{25}}{-\frac{17}{25}} = \frac{4\sqrt{21}}{17}$
$\displaystyle \sin 2u = -\frac{4\sqrt{21}}{25}, \quad \cos 2u = -\frac{17}{25}, \quad \tan 2u = \frac{4\sqrt{21}}{17}$
✔ Book answer A210

Section 4: Rewrite in Terms of $x$ and Composites

Let each inverse function be an angle, draw its right triangle, then expand.

Pg 385 #45Sum/Diff
Write the trigonometric expression as an algebraic expression: $\sin(\arctan 2x - \arccos x)$.
Formula used: $\sin(u \pm v) = \sin u\cos v \pm \cos u\sin v$, $\ \cos(u \pm v) = \cos u\cos v \mp \sin u\sin v$, $\ \tan(u \pm v) = \frac{\tan u \pm \tan v}{1 \mp \tan u\tan v}$
Show steps and answer

Let $u = \arctan 2x$: opposite $2x$, adjacent $1$, hypotenuse $\sqrt{4x^2 + 1}$. So $\sin u = \frac{2x}{\sqrt{4x^2 + 1}}$ and $\cos u = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4x^2 + 1}}$.

Let $v = \arccos x$: adjacent $x$, hypotenuse $1$, opposite $\sqrt{1 - x^2}$. So $\cos v = x$ and $\sin v = \sqrt{1 - x^2}$.

$\displaystyle \sin(u - v) = \sin u\cos v - \cos u\sin v = \frac{2x}{\sqrt{4x^2 + 1}}\cdot x - \frac{1}{\sqrt{4x^2 + 1}}\cdot\sqrt{1 - x^2}$
$\displaystyle = \frac{2x^2 - \sqrt{1 - x^2}}{\sqrt{4x^2 + 1}}$
$\displaystyle \frac{2x^2 - \sqrt{1 - x^2}}{\sqrt{4x^2 + 1}}$
✔ Book answer A210
Pg 385 #61Sum/Diff
Use right triangles to evaluate the expression: $\sin\left(\tan^{-1}\frac{3}{4} + \sin^{-1}\frac{3}{5}\right)$.
Formula used: $\sin(u \pm v) = \sin u\cos v \pm \cos u\sin v$, $\ \cos(u \pm v) = \cos u\cos v \mp \sin u\sin v$, $\ \tan(u \pm v) = \frac{\tan u \pm \tan v}{1 \mp \tan u\tan v}$
Show steps and answer

Let $u = \tan^{-1}\frac{3}{4}$: opposite $3$, adjacent $4$, hypotenuse $5$. So $\sin u = \frac{3}{5}$ and $\cos u = \frac{4}{5}$.

Let $v = \sin^{-1}\frac{3}{5}$: opposite $3$, hypotenuse $5$, adjacent $4$. So $\sin v = \frac{3}{5}$ and $\cos v = \frac{4}{5}$.

$\displaystyle \sin(u + v) = \sin u\cos v + \cos u\sin v = \frac{3}{5}\cdot\frac{4}{5} + \frac{4}{5}\cdot\frac{3}{5} = \frac{24}{25}$
$\displaystyle \frac{24}{25}$
✔ Book answer A210
Pg 396 #115Double Angle
Write the trigonometric expression as an algebraic expression: $\sin(2\arcsin x)$.
Formula used: Double angle: $\sin 2u = 2\sin u\cos u$
Show steps and answer

Let $u = \arcsin x$: opposite $x$, hypotenuse $1$, adjacent $\sqrt{1 - x^2}$. So $\sin u = x$ and $\cos u = \sqrt{1 - x^2}$.

$\displaystyle \sin(2\arcsin x) = \sin 2u = 2\sin u\cos u = 2x\sqrt{1 - x^2}$
$\displaystyle 2x\sqrt{1 - x^2}$
